# Create.WitUS Code of Conduct

**Version 1.0** · effective at launch

> **Implementation note (not part of the member-facing text):** this version string is stored on
> `application.coc_version_accepted` at signup. Bumping the **major** version requires
> re-acceptance from every member before they can post a call, express interest, or send a
> message. Minor versions (typos, clarifications) do not. Keep the changelog at the bottom.

---

## Why you're reading this

Create.WitUS exists so people can find each other and make things. That only works if it's a
place people feel safe enough to show up to. These rules are short, and they're enforced.

By joining, you agree to all of it.

## 1. This is not a dating service

Don't use Create.WitUS to look for dates, hookups, or romance. Don't treat someone's interest in
your project as interest in you. Don't send flirtatious or sexual messages, and don't comment on
anyone's appearance.

This is the rule we will enforce most bluntly, because the failure mode is obvious and it ruins
the place for everyone else. Using this platform as a dating app gets you removed, usually on
the first report.

If you and a collaborator become close, that's your business — off this platform.

## 2. You must be 18 or older

No exceptions in the current version. If we learn an account belongs to someone under 18, we
close it.

## 3. Show up as yourself, honestly

- Your work samples must be **your work**. Collaborative work is fine — say what your part was.
- Don't misrepresent your skill level, your training, or your experience. Nobody cares if you're
  a beginner; everybody cares if you said you weren't.
- Don't claim certifications, licenses, or safety training you don't hold. If someone is
  deciding whether to hand you a tool, this one matters more than your pride.
- One account per person.

## 4. Harassment gets you removed

No harassment, threats, stalking, or intimidation. No slurs or demeaning treatment based on
race, ethnicity, national origin, immigration status, religion, disability, age, body, gender,
gender identity or expression, or sexual orientation.

**"No" is a complete answer.** If someone declines your interest, doesn't reply, or ends a
conversation, that's the end of it. Don't ask again through another call. Don't make a new
account. Repeat contact after a decline is harassment, and we treat it that way.

Don't share anyone's personal information — address, phone number, employer, immigration status,
anything — without their clear permission. Never as retaliation.

## 5. Respect people's work

- Credit collaborators. Agree on credit **before** you start, not after it goes well.
- Don't post someone else's work as a sample, and don't repost work from a call or profile
  without permission.
- Talk about ownership, licensing, and rights early. "We'll figure it out later" is how
  friendships end.
- If a collaboration produces something you both worked on, neither of you gets to publish it as
  solely yours.

## 6. Be honest about money and time

- Say up front whether a call is **paid, unpaid, a split, or barter**. Mark it accurately.
- Don't advertise something as "exposure" or "for your portfolio" and later expect professional
  output on a deadline.
- If you commit, show up. If you can't, say so as early as you can — a cancellation is
  disappointing, a no-show is disrespectful.
- Don't recruit for MLMs, run scams, ask for money up front, or pitch unrelated services.

## 7. Meeting in person

Some calls happen in the physical world. **Create.WitUS provides no space, no tools, no
insurance, no supervision, and no vetting of anyone.** We don't check identities, run background
checks, or verify that anyone is who they say they are. Everyone you meet here is a stranger.

If you meet in person:

- **Meet in public first** when you can, before going to a private studio, shop, or home.
- **Tell someone** where you're going, who you're meeting, and when you expect to be back.
- **Trust your gut** and leave whenever you want. You never owe anyone an explanation, a
  session, or a reason.
- **Around tools and machines**, don't let anyone pressure you into equipment you haven't been
  trained on, and don't pressure anyone else. A level on a profile is self-declared — it is not
  a qualification, and we don't verify it.
- **Talk about safety and liability before you start**, especially anything involving power
  tools, heat, heights, chemicals, or vehicles.

## 8. What's not allowed at all

Sexual content, content sexualizing minors, gratuitous violence, hate content, content
promoting self-harm, illegal activity, weapons manufacture, and anything you don't hold the
rights to.

## 9. Reporting

Every profile, call, and message has **Report** and **Block**.

- **Block** is immediate and needs no explanation. You and the person you blocked disappear from
  each other's feeds, searches, and call lists.
- **Report** goes to a human. You'll get an acknowledgement. We may not be able to tell you the
  outcome — privacy cuts both ways — but every report is read.
- **False reports made to harass someone are themselves a violation.**

**If you are in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services first.** We're a website;
we can close an account, and that's all we can do.

## 10. What happens when someone breaks the rules

Depending on severity and history: a warning · content removal · suspension · permanent removal.

We skip straight to permanent removal for threats, sexual content involving minors, doxxing,
stalking, and anything that puts someone in physical danger. There's no strike system for those.

Decisions are made by a human. In the current version that human is the operator; as the
community grows, moderation roles will be shared, and this document will be updated to say
exactly how. **You can appeal any decision by replying to the notice you receive.**

## 11. Changes

We'll update this as the community teaches us what we missed. Material changes require you to
accept the new version before you can post, express interest, or message.
